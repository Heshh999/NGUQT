## 0. Where this comes from, and what was already seen

The operator asked, on 2026-11-27, for a search of what is known about
forecasting which way the market goes and for how long. The pages
could not be opened from here; what follows was read in search
summaries only.

- **The one direction forecast with a reason behind it is a flow.**
  Option market makers who are short gamma, and leveraged funds, which
  rebalance at the end of the day in proportion to the day's move, have
  to trade in the direction the day has gone, near the close
  (Baltussen, Da, Lammers and Martens, "Hedging demand and market
  intraday momentum", 2021: in more than 60 futures from 1974 to 2020
  the last half hour follows the rest of the day; on the S&P 500 only
  when the dealers' gamma is negative; it gives back over the next
  days).
- **B's own authors tie their rule to it** (Concretum, "Do Leveraged
  ETFs Fuel Intraday Trends?": intraday momentum works considerably
  better where leveraged-fund activity is intense). Trade press reports
  those flows at a record size in 2026.
- **Independent re-runs of B's source** reproduce it on its own sample,
  find it weaker since its publication in 2024, and one finds it on NQ
  futures and not on ES.

If those flows are what B earns from, B's result should sit late in
the day, more so on NQ. A B that trades only late would then keep most
of the result with a third of the time in the market, and with
smaller falls, which is what the account plan on NQ's ten years
lacked.

**Seen beforehand:** everything in the reports of waves twenty-four and
twenty-six on NQ's ten years (B's result by halves; the account by
year); the outside results above. **Wave twenty-four's C** (the last
half hour follows the day, on every day) failed the screen on NQ.

**Not seen:** B's result by time of day, or by the age of its position,

## 2. The legs of B (reported, never judged)

B decides at 10:00, 10:30, …, 15:30 and is flat at P(16:00). A **leg**
is one half hour of that clock, from a decision time to the next:
twelve legs, 10:00–10:30 to 15:30–16:00.

- **A leg's result** on a session = the position B holds in it × the
  change of P over it ÷ O, less the costs charged to it.
- **Costs:** an order pays B's cost on the size traded. What closes the
  old position is charged to the leg that ends at the order; what opens
  the new one, to the leg that starts at it. The legs of a session add
  up to B's result on it.
- **EARLY** = the eight legs from 10:00 to 14:00. **LATE** = the four
  legs from 14:00 to 16:00.
- **A leg's age** = the number of half hours since the position's
  direction was last set; the leg that starts at the order has age 1.
  Reported by age 1, 2, 3–4, and 5 or more.

Reported for each leg, each group and each age: the sessions in a
position, the mean result a session, its share of B's whole result,
and its weekly t.

## 3. Two things written down beforehand

- **LATE-HEAVY** holds on a market when B's whole result there is above
  0 and the LATE legs earn **more than half** of it. They are a third
  of the clock.
- **NQ-MORE** holds when the LATE legs' mean result a session is larger
  on NQ than on ES and than on RTY.

Each is reported as HELD or NOT HELD, per market and together. Beside:
LATE's result less half of EARLY's, session by session (zero when a
half hour earns the same early and late), with its weekly t.

## 4. BL — B from 14:00

B's rule with decisions only at **14:00, 14:30, 15:00 and 15:30**: flat
before 14:00, flat at P(16:00). At 14:00 it takes the position B's rule
asks for then, whenever the price left the bounds. Nothing else
changes.

B's position at a decision time depends only on the prices at that
time, so BL holds what B holds from 14:00 on; it differs from B's LATE
legs only by the cost of entering at 14:00 a position B already had.

## 5. The verdict on BL

With **Sharpe** = the mean of a session's result over its standard
deviation, and the **fall ratio** = the largest drop of the running sum
from its highest point over the mean (wave thirty-one's numbers), BL is
**BETTER THAN B** when all of these hold:

- BL passes wave twenty-four's bar on each of NQ, ES and RTY;
- on **each of ES and RTY**, B's Sharpe is above 0, BL's Sharpe is **at
  least 5 % higher** than B's, and BL's fall ratio is lower than B's;
- in NQ's later months (2026-07-01 to 2026-11-13) BL is not COLLAPSED
  (wave twenty-five's verdicts).

Otherwise it is **NOT BETTER**: B stays as it is, and no other starting
hour is tried on these bars. A B with a third of the time in the market
has a lower Sharpe than B unless the late legs carry well over half of
the result, so with no effect a false pass is rare: about one such
study in thirty.

**What goes on.** BL takes B's place in every later step when it is
better than B, **but only if B itself passes wave twenty-five**. The
legs and the two statements of §3 lead to nothing by themselves.

## Expected beforehand (written 2026-11-27, before anything was computed)

B is in a position a similar share of the time early and late, and a
trend that lasts hours earns in every half hour it is held. The late
legs are probably somewhat richer than a third: perhaps two fifths of
the result. **LATE-HEAVY:** on NQ about one chance in three; on ES or
RTY, where B has a profit at all, about one in four. **NQ-MORE:** about
one chance in two. **BL better than B:** about one chance in seven. The
most likely outcome: B earns through the day, a little more late, and
is better left whole.
